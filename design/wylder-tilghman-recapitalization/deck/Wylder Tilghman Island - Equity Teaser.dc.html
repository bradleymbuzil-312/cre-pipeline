Wylder Hotel Tilghman Island is a waterfront resort on Tilghman Island, at the mouth of the Choptank River on Maryland’s Eastern Shore. It is open, operating and under the Wylder Hotels brand, and it is part of the way through a repositioning that the sponsor has been funding from its own capital.

What remains is the part that changes the economics: a full renovation of the guest rooms, a rebuilt and expanded marina, a replaced residence, reworked public space, and an adjacent waterfront parcel under contract that brings further slips and a private whole‑home suite into the resort.

The sponsor is seeking equity to complete that work alongside the capital it has already committed. This package sets out the three things that matter to that decision and states no figures; the model, the operating history and the full capitalization follow under a confidentiality agreement.

What Underpins It
Waterfront cannot be added to

There is a finite quantity of improved deep‑water frontage on the mid‑Shore, and essentially none of it is being created. A competing resort cannot be built across the road; it has to be bought.

The marina is its own asset

Slips are valued on their own terms, against slip sales and slip rents in the surrounding creeks, and they carry almost no operating overhead. The adjacent parcel roughly doubles the slip count.

A basis set by cost, not by trade

The sponsor’s basis reflects what it has paid to assemble and improve the property. A stabilized waterfront resort on the Bay does not change hands on that measure.

Income, once it is there

On completion the asset is valued the way resorts are valued — on what it earns. Every argument in the two sections that follow is an argument about that number.

Driver One
Rate

The rooms are being sold today at a rate a renovated waterfront resort does not accept. The comparable set on this stretch of shore holds a materially higher rate the whole season. The renovation is what closes that gap; the specification is already built and can be inspected.

Driver Two
Inventory

Rooms that have sat out of service come back, and further keys are added within the existing footprint. That is inventory the property already owns, already heats and already insures, and has never been able to sell.

Driver Three
Departments

The marina, the bar, the event space and the whole‑home residences are either new or being rebuilt. They carry very little operating overhead, so what they bring in largely stays in.

The Wylder brand

The property already trades under Wylder Hotels, a boutique brand that buys, restores and operates resorts in drive‑to leisure markets. It is not being rebranded; it is being finished.

A soft‑brand affiliation, on completion

A Small Luxury Hotels affiliation is planned once the renovation is done. SLH member hotels are bookable through Hilton’s site, app and loyalty program, which moves the resort out of dependence on direct booking and the online travel agents.

A relationship that already works

The sponsor’s sister resort in the Catskills is an SLH member — the first in that market — and carries the American Express Fine Hotels & Resorts designation. This is an extension of a working relationship, not a cold application.

Why it matters here

Rate on the Eastern Shore is made in the shoulder months, and the shoulder months are made by distribution. The affiliation is the mechanism behind the rate and occupancy the plan assumes.

Affiliation is targeted on completion of the renovation and remains subject to application, inspection, board approval and an executed brand agreement.

Structure and Alignment
Owner‑operated

Day‑to‑day operations and the renovation are run by an affiliate of ownership. There is no third‑party management agreement to terminate and no split incentive between the owner and the operator.

Continuous ownership

The sponsor has owned and operated this hotel under the Wylder brand through the pandemic cycle and the current repositioning, and has put further capital in along the way.

A single‑asset entity

The ownership entity holds this property and, on closing, the adjacent parcel — and nothing else — keeping the asset and its cash flow ring‑fenced.

A platform behind it

Revenue management, distribution, design and procurement are shared across the two Wylder resorts, giving a property of this size a reach it could not support standing alone.

The Operator
Wylder Hotels

A boutique brand that acquires, renovates and operates resorts in drive‑to leisure markets, founded and led by John Flannigan. Before Wylder he was chief operating officer of Proper Hospitality and president of JRK Hotels, across roughly three decades in hospitality.

The Proof Case

The sister resort in the Catskills opened after a substantial program of investment and was named the leading resort in New York State the following year. It is a Small Luxury Hotels member, the first in that market, holds the American Express Fine Hotels & Resorts designation, sits among Travel + Leisure’s World’s Best and is a MICHELIN Guide selection.

Tilghman Island follows the same path — the same ownership, the same management company, the same brand platform and the same affiliation route — on an asset with water on both sides and a marina the Catskills could never have.
