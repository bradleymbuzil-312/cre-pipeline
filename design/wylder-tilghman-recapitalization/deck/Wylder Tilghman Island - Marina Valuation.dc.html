What the Slips Are

These are hotel amenity slips, not a marina business. There is no boatyard, no dry stack, no service bays and no fuel operation across either property. That is why the slip-focused trades are the direct evidence and the full-service sales are context only.

The Wylder slips are rebuilt inside the $250,000 marina line of the renovation program, about $10,870 per existing slip. Scope, permits and whether the slips are rebuilt or replaced all need confirming.

These four are context, not slip prices. Each price bought a business as well as frontage: rooms and restaurants at Lowe’s Wharf and St. Michaels, a full-service yard and dry storage at Bachelor Point and Bay Bridge. The subject has none of that.

What They Prove, and What They Do Not
Business Value Is Local and Real

Lowe’s Wharf, ten minutes from the subject, sold at $184,091 a slip in August 2024 because the price bought ten rooms, a restaurant and bar, a six-bedroom house and a fuel operation on 2.5 acres. It went at 81% of a $5.0M ask.

Institutional Buyers Pay for Yards

Bachelor Point at $125,000 and Bay Bridge at $94,025 are both Hinckley acquisitions of full-service yards with dry and heated storage. Bachelor Point had sold for $5.0M in 2015, a 3.4% annual gain over ten years.

None of It Transfers to Amenity Slips

The subject’s 47 slips carry no dry stack, no service bays and no fuel business. Pricing them off these trades would be valuing a business that does not exist here. They are included because they price this shoreline, not because they set the number.

The Premium Has to Be Earned

The model’s $7,645 a slip is about 1.7 times the market rent assumption. That only holds if hotel guests and transient boaters fill the slips at nightly rates, which is plausible for a resort marina but unproven here.

Upside, Not Collateral

At the underwriter expense load the model case still indicates $2,747,553 to $3,504,188, or $58,459 to $74,557 a slip — well above the recommendation. It is treated as upside to be proven, not value to lend against.

What Would Prove It

A trailing three-year marina profit and loss statement. It was promised in earlier lender questions and is still the document that decides whether this case is credible. Until it exists the sales approach governs.

Risks and open items.

Slip Counts

Confirm 23 and 24 against a survey, the Dogwood Cove purchase and sale agreement and the riparian permits. Every value here scales linearly with the count.

Commercial Use of the Dogwood Cove Slips

Using the 24 slips commercially as part of the hotel needs Talbot County zoning, Critical Area and state environmental confirmation. This is the difference between about $280,000 and $720,000.

Marina Rebuild

The $250,000 marina line is about $10,870 per existing slip. Confirm scope, permits, and whether the 23 slips are rebuilt in place or replaced.

Historical Marina Revenue

The three-year marina schedule is the key open document. It decides whether the sponsor model case is credible.

Cap Rate and Expense Load

Both are assumptions. Published marina cap rates run roughly 8% to 14%; the expense load is unverified for this operation. A marina specialist broker opinion would firm both up.

Comp Verification

Deed prices come from the state assessment file. Multiple-parcel deeds may include land beyond the marina, and one directory address should be reconciled against the recorded parcel. Slip counts come from press releases and directories. The 3% time adjustment is an assumption.
